Sourdough loaf

Starter
Feed the starter twice a day at room temperature, at a ratio of 1:5:5 (starter, flour, water by weight). If you bake less than once a week, keep it in the fridge and feed it once a week instead, then give it two room-temperature feeds before baking.

Dough
The dough is 500 g bread flour, 350 g water, 100 g active starter and 10 g salt. Mix the flour and water first and leave them for 45 minutes (the autolyse) before adding the starter and salt.

Bulk fermentation
Do four sets of stretch and folds, 30 minutes apart. Then leave the dough until it has risen by about half, which takes 4 to 6 hours at 24 degrees C. It should look domed and jiggle when you move the bowl.

Shaping and proofing
Shape the dough into a tight ball, put it seam side up in a floured banneton and leave it in the fridge overnight, 12 to 16 hours. The cold proof makes the loaf easier to score and gives a more sour flavour.

Baking
Preheat the oven with the Dutch oven inside for 45 minutes. Bake in a Dutch oven at 250 degrees C for 20 minutes with the lid on, then take the lid off, lower the oven to 230 degrees C and bake 20 to 25 minutes more, until the crust is deep brown.

Troubleshooting
A dense, gummy crumb usually means the dough was underproofed: let bulk fermentation run longer next time. A flat loaf that spreads out usually means it was overproofed or shaped too loosely.
